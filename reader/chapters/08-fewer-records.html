08 · Compress the sequence

Eviction deletes positions. Compression merges each run of m tokens into one learned record, so the cache grows as N/m instead of N.

Compressive Transformer: pool old memories, don’t drop them

Compressive Transformer 2019 · Rae et al., DeepMind · ICLR 2020 · arXiv:1911.05507 · Used in: research only; the ancestor of the HCA idea, and the paper that introduced PG-19

cm←fc(moldest ns)∈ℝ⌊ns/c⌋×d,range=L(nm+cncm) \text{cm}\leftarrow f_c\big(m_{\text{oldest }n_s}\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{\lfloor n_s/c\rfloor\times d},\qquad \text{range}=L\,(n_m+c\,n_{cm})

  • Why it works: pooling old memories at rate cc instead of dropping them lets a fixed slot budget cover cc times more history.
  • Compare: it reads every pooled record densely, like HCA, with no top-k selection, unlike CSA.
  • Converts from: the “most-used” fcf_c keeps the most-attended memories, which is H2O-style eviction.
  • Numbers (reported): 0.97 bpc on Enwik8 and 17.1 ppl on WikiText-103. On PG-19, 33.6 ppl vs 36.3 for Transformer-XL.

Gist tokens → Activation Beacon: learned summary tokens

Gist tokens 2023 · Mu, Li, Goodman, Stanford · NeurIPS 2023 · arXiv:2304.08467 — Activation Beacon 2024 · Zhang et al., BAAI / Renmin University · ICLR 2025 · arXiv:2401.03462 · Used in: research (FlagEmbedding release)

gist: Mij=0for i>G,j∈P;beacon: |KV|≈Nα+w,α∈{2,4,8,16,32} \text{gist: } M_{ij}=0\ \ \text{for } i>G,\ j\in P;\qquad \text{beacon: } |\text{KV}|\approx\frac{N}{\alpha}+w,\quad \alpha\in\{2,4,8,16,32\}

  • Learned records: special tokens are trained to absorb a span. The span’s own KV is then dropped.
  • Gist vs Beacon: gist squeezes one prompt into a few tokens. Beacon compresses every chunk of a long input, in every layer.
  • Train vs. infer: Beacon trains only new beacon projections and keeps the LLM frozen. A random α\alpha per chunk lets one model serve several ratios.
  • Not solved: both are added after pretraining. CSA and HCA build compression into pretraining.

NSA’s compression branch: the bridge

NSA 2025 · Yuan et al., DeepSeek / Peking University · ACL 2025 · arXiv:2502.11089 · Used in: research (27B MoE in the paper); full card in 06

K̃tcmp={φ(kid+1:id+l):0≤i≤⌊t−ld⌋},l=32,d=16 \tilde K^{\text{cmp}}_t=\Big\{\varphi\big(k_{id+1:\,id+l}\big)\;:\;0\le i\le\Big\lfloor\frac{t-l}{d}\Big\rfloor\Big\},\qquad l=32,\ d=16

  • Bridge: overlapping windows (l=2dl = 2d) and a learned pooler φ\varphi are the two ingredients CSA keeps.
  • Two jobs: compressed keys are read densely. Their scores, summed per 64-token block and per GQA group, pick the selected blocks.
  • Not solved: the raw KV stays for the selection and window branches, so storage grows. CSA drops it.
  • Numbers: at 64K a query reads about 4,095 compressed keys, 16 × 64 selected tokens and a 512 window: 8.6% of the context.

CSA: compress by 4, then select top-k

CSA (Compressed Sparse Attention) 2026 · DeepSeek-AI · arXiv:2606.19348 · Used in: DeepSeek-V4-Flash, DeepSeek-V4-Pro

ci=∑j∈blki−1∪blkisoftmax⁡j(zj+Bj)⊙xj,At=Top⁡k(It,:)∪win128,|blk|=m=4 c_i=\sum_{j\in\text{blk}_{i-1}\cup\,\text{blk}_i}\operatorname{softmax}_j\big(z_j+B_j\big)\odot x_j,\qquad A_t=\operatorname{Top}_k\big(I_{t,:}\big)\cup\text{win}_{128},\qquad |\text{blk}|=m=4

  • Two budgets at once: stored records drop to N/4N/4, and reads are capped at kk = 1,024 (Pro) or 512 (Flash).
  • Overlap: entry ii sees blocks i−1i-1 and ii through separate projections, yet one entry is emitted per 4 tokens.
  • Indexer: DSA-style, 64 heads × 128 dims, with QK cached and multiplied in FP4. kk is below V3.2’s 2,048 because entries span several tokens.
  • Storage: one 512-dim entry serves as both K and V. RoPE dims stay BF16, the rest FP8. Each head has a sink logit.

HCA: compress by 128, read everything

HCA (Heavily Compressed Attention) 2026 · DeepSeek-AI · arXiv:2606.19348 · Used in: DeepSeek-V4-Flash, DeepSeek-V4-Pro

ci=∑j∈blkisoftmax⁡j(zj+Bj)⊙xj,|blki|=m′=128,At={cs:s<⌊t/128⌋}∪win128 c_i=\sum_{j\in\text{blk}_i}\operatorname{softmax}_j\big(z_j+B_j\big)\odot x_j,\quad |\text{blk}_i|=m'=128,\qquad A_t=\{c_s: s<\lfloor t/128\rfloor\}\cup\text{win}_{128}

  • No scorer: every finished entry is read, so there is no indexer cost and no top-k miss.
  • Cost: reads still grow as N/128N/128. At 1M a query reads 8,320 entries, against CSA’s fixed 1,152.
  • Why pair them: CSA keeps finer entries but must choose among them. HCA sees all of history, coarsely. V4 alternates the two by layer.
  • Compare: the Compressive Transformer also read pooled memories densely, with cc = 2–4 and a bounded memory.

DeepSeek-V4: layer layout and bytes per token

KV bytes/token=∑ℓbentrymℓ,bentry=448⋅1B (FP8)+64⋅2B (BF16 RoPE)=576B \text{KV bytes/token}=\sum_{\ell}\frac{b_{\text{entry}}}{m_\ell},\qquad b_{\text{entry}}=448\cdot 1\ \text{B (FP8)}+64\cdot 2\ \text{B (BF16 RoPE)}=576\ \text{B}

  • Layouts differ: “starts with two HCA layers” is true only for V4-Pro. V4-Flash starts with two sliding-window-only layers.
  • Read the config: compress_ratios lists mm for each layer: 0 = window only, 4 = CSA, 128 = HCA.
  • CSA dominates: CSA layers hold about 97% of Pro’s bytes per token. Each HCA layer adds only 4.5 B.
  • Numbers (reported): at 1M, Pro needs 27% of V3.2’s per-token FLOPs and 10% of its KV. Flash needs 10% and 7%.
