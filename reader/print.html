00 · The map

Every mechanism in this book attacks one factor of the attention bill. Here are the bill, the levers, and when each lever appeared.

The bill: prefill pays N2N^2, decode pays NN bytes per token

FLOPprefill≈2N2d⋅Lbytesdecode step≈N⋅L⋅2Hkvdh⋅s+weights \text{FLOP}_{\text{prefill}}\approx 2N^2d\cdot L \qquad \text{bytes}_{\text{decode step}}\approx N\cdot L\cdot 2H_{kv}d_h\cdot s\;+\;\text{weights}

  • Two different walls. Long prompts hit compute (prefill). Long generations and big batches hit HBM bandwidth (decode).
  • At 128K the cache outweighs the model. Llama-3-8B reads more KV bytes than weight bytes per token.
  • Batch multiplies KV, not weights. That is why serving cares about cache bytes first.
Where the numbers come from

Llama-3-8B: L=32L=32, Hqdh=4096H_q d_h = 4096, Hkv=8H_{kv}=8, dh=128d_h=128, 8.03 B parameters, BF16. Causal prefill: 2N2d2N^2 d per layer =2⋅1310722⋅4096⋅32≈4.5×1015= 2\cdot 131072^2\cdot 4096\cdot 32 \approx 4.5\times10^{15}. The rest of the model: 2⋅8.03×109⋅131072≈2.1×10152\cdot 8.03\times10^9\cdot 131072\approx 2.1\times10^{15}. KV: 32⋅2⋅8⋅128⋅232\cdot 2\cdot 8\cdot 128\cdot 2 B =128= 128 KiB per token, times 131,072 = 16 GiB.

Seven levers

decode bytes≈Nread⏟05–08⋅wrecord⏟03–04⋅L⋅s⏟13–14or|S|⋅L⋅s⏟09–12 \text{decode bytes}\approx \underbrace{N_{\text{read}}}_{\text{05–08}}\cdot\underbrace{w_{\text{record}}}_{\text{03–04}}\cdot L\cdot \underbrace{s}_{\text{13–14}} \qquad\text{or}\qquad \underbrace{|S|\cdot L\cdot s}_{\text{09–12}}

  • Reads vs records. Sparse reads (06) keep the cache and touch less. Eviction (07) and compression (08) shrink the cache itself.
  • State models change the formula. Cost stops growing with NN; capacity becomes the question (09–11).
  • Production stacks combine levers. DeepSeek-V4: compressed records + top-k sparse reads + a local window + FP8 storage.

01 · Positions

RoPE decides how far apart two tokens look. Long context needs slow pairs that span the window, rescaled pairs, or no rotation at all.

RoPE: a spectrum of clocks, set by the base

RoPE 2021 · Su et al. · arXiv:2104.09864 — ABF 2023 · Xiong et al., Meta · arXiv:2309.16039 · Used in: Llama 3 (b = 500K), gpt-oss (150K), Qwen3 (1M), Qwen3-Next (10M), Gemma 3 (10K local / 1M global)

θi=b−2i/dh,λi=2πθi=2πb2i/dh,(Rtq)⊤(Rjk)=q⊤Rj−tk \theta_i=b^{-2i/d_h},\qquad \lambda_i=\frac{2\pi}{\theta_i}=2\pi\,b^{2i/d_h},\qquad (R_t q)^{\top}(R_j k)=q^{\top}R_{j-t}\,k

  • Why raise b: a pair’s angle repeats every λi\lambda_i tokens. ABF (Llama 2 Long: 10K → 500K) slows every pair and weakens long-distance decay.
  • Numbers (dh=128d_h=128): slowest wavelength 54K tokens at b = 10K, 2.6M at 500K, 5.1M at 1M. Fastest is always 2π ≈ 6.3.
  • Dual bases: local layers never see distances beyond W, so they keep b = 10K and skip scaling (Gemma 3, DeepSeek-V4 SWA layers).
  • Not solved: a larger base is not extrapolation. Positions past the trained length still need scaling (next card).

Context extension: PI → NTK-aware → YaRN / llama3

PI 2023 · Chen et al., Meta · arXiv:2306.15595 — YaRN 2023 · Peng et al., Nous Research / EleutherAI · ICLR 2024 · arXiv:2309.00071 · Used in: gpt-oss, DeepSeek-V3, DeepSeek-V4, OLMo 3, Qwen3 (optional); llama3 type: Llama 3.1, Llama 4

θ′i=(1−γ(ri))θis+γ(ri)θi,ri=Lλi,γ(r)=clip⁡(r−αβ−α,0,1) \theta'_i=\big(1-\gamma(r_i)\big)\frac{\theta_i}{s}+\gamma(r_i)\,\theta_i,\qquad r_i=\frac{L}{\lambda_i},\qquad \gamma(r)=\operatorname{clip}\Big(\frac{r-\alpha}{\beta-\alpha},0,1\Big)

  • Compare: PI divides every pair by ss and blurs local order. YaRN keeps pairs with λi<L/β\lambda_i < L/\beta and interpolates only slow ones.
  • Numbers: gpt-oss 4,096 → 131,072 (s = 32). DeepSeek-V3 4,096 → 163,840 (s = 40). Llama 3.1 8,192 → 131,072 (llama3, s = 8).
  • Temperature is free: logits × (0.1ln⁡s+1)2(0.1\ln s+1)^2, folded into cos/sin or the softmax scale. The llama3 type has none.
  • Variants: LongRoPE searches one factor per pair (Phi-4-mini: 1.0 → 47.8). Dual Chunk Attention remaps distances per chunk, training-free (Qwen3 inference).

Partial RoPE and p-RoPE: rotate only some dims

Partial RoPE 2022 · Black et al., EleutherAI (GPT-NeoX-20B) · arXiv:2204.06745 — p-RoPE 2024 · Barbero et al., Oxford / Google DeepMind · ICLR 2025 · arXiv:2410.06205 · Used in: GLM-4.5, Qwen3-Next, MiniMax-M2, MiMo-V2-Flash, Phi-4-mini, Gemma 4, DeepSeek-V4

qt⊤kj→(Rtqtrot)⊤(Rjkjrot)+(qtnope)⊤kjnope,drot=pdh q_t^{\top}k_j\;\to\;(R_t\,q^{\text{rot}}_t)^{\top}(R_j\,k^{\text{rot}}_j)+(q^{\text{nope}}_t)^{\top}k^{\text{nope}}_j,\qquad d_{\text{rot}}=p\,d_h

  • Compare: NeoX-style partial squeezes the whole spectrum into pdhp\,d_h dims. p-RoPE keeps the fastest pp of the full spectrum and freezes the rest (θ = 0).
  • Why it works: slow pairs barely turn within the context and already act as content channels. p-RoPE makes them exactly position-free.
  • Cache link: unrotated dims are position-free, so they can be absorbed or shared: MLA’s decoupled RoPE lane, K=V in Gemma 4 and DeepSeek-V4.

NoPE layers: iRoPE and RNoPE

NoPE 2023 · Kazemnejad et al. · NeurIPS 2023 · arXiv:2305.19466 — iRoPE 2025 · Meta (Llama 4) — RNoPE 2025 · Yang et al., Cohere · arXiv:2501.18795 · Used in: Llama 4, Command A, SmolLM3

etjRoPE=(Rtqt)⊤(Rjkj)dh+mtjlocal,etjNoPE=qt⊤kjdh+mtjcausal e^{\text{RoPE}}_{tj}=\frac{(R_t q_t)^{\top}(R_j k_j)}{\sqrt{d_h}}+m^{\text{local}}_{tj},\qquad e^{\text{NoPE}}_{tj}=\frac{q_t^{\top}k_j}{\sqrt{d_h}}+m^{\text{causal}}_{tj}

  • Why it works: the causal mask alone leaks order. RoPE layers supply recency; NoPE layers retrieve distant tokens (RNoPE §2).
  • Cache: Llama 4’s RoPE layers are chunked, so only 12 of 48 layers grow with N.
  • Gotcha: raising θ in the hybrid’s RoPE layers hurt retrieval. Needle attention in NoPE layers fell 0.077 → 0.037 (reported).
  • Not solved: NoPE logits flatten as context grows. Llama 4 adds a temperature to exactly these layers (next card).

Length-dependent logit temperature

SSMax 2025 · Nakanishi · arXiv:2501.19399 — Attention temperature tuning 2025 · Meta (Llama 4 config) · Used in: Llama 4 (NoPE layers); YaRN models use a constant factor

softmax⁡j(τetj),τLlama 4(t)=1+0.1ln⁡(1+⌊t+18192⌋),τSSMax=shln⁡n \operatorname{softmax}_j\big(\tau\,e_{tj}\big),\qquad \tau_{\text{Llama 4}}(t)=1+0.1\ln\Big(1+\Big\lfloor\frac{t+1}{8192}\Big\rfloor\Big),\qquad \tau_{\text{SSMax}}=s_h\ln n

  • Why: with nn keys and a fixed gap Δ, top weight ≈ eΔ/ne^{\Delta}/n. Attention fades as context grows (SSMax §2).
  • Numbers: Llama 4 τ = 1.07 at 8K, 1.28 at 128K, 1.72 at 10M (attn_scale 0.1, floor_scale 8,192).
  • Compare: SSMax learns shs_h per layer and head (144 parameters at 162M). YaRN applies one constant to every position.
  • History · ALiBi (ICLR 2022): no rotation; subtract mh(t−j)m_h(t-j), slopes mh=2−8h/Hm_h=2^{-8h/H}. Trained on 1,024 tokens, it matched sinusoidal trained on 2,048.

02 · Stable softmax

Softmax must sum to one, and its logits can grow without bound. Modern layers cap the logits, give each head an exit, and gate its output.

QK-norm, soft-cap, QK-Clip: bound the logits

QK-norm 2023 · Dehghani et al. (ViT-22B); logit-growth study Wortsman et al., Google DeepMind · ICLR 2024 · arXiv:2309.14322 — Soft-cap 2024 · Gemma 2 · arXiv:2408.00118 — QK-Clip 2025 · Kimi K2 · arXiv:2507.20534 · Used in: Qwen3, Qwen3-Next, OLMo 2/3, Gemma 3/4, GLM-4.5, MiniMax-M2, Llama 4 Scout, DeepSeek-V4

q̂=gq⊙qRMS⁡(q),k̂=gk⊙kRMS⁡(k)⇒|q̂⊤k̂dh|≤dh∥gq∥∞∥gk∥∞ \hat q=g_q\odot\frac{q}{\operatorname{RMS}(q)},\quad \hat k=g_k\odot\frac{k}{\operatorname{RMS}(k)}\;\Rightarrow\;\Big|\frac{\hat q^{\top}\hat k}{\sqrt{d_h}}\Big|\le\sqrt{d_h}\,\|g_q\|_\infty\|g_k\|_\infty

  • Why logits grow: 1/dh1/\sqrt{d_h} fixes the scale only at initialisation. Trained ‖q‖‖k‖ grow until attention turns one-hot (Wortsman §3.1).
  • Variants: soft-cap e←ctanh⁡(e/c)e\leftarrow c\tanh(e/c) shapes logits in the forward pass. QK-Clip rescales Wq,WkW_q,W_k by γh\sqrt{\gamma_h}, γh=min⁡(1,τ/Smax⁡h)\gamma_h=\min(1,\tau/S^h_{\max}), after the optimizer step.
  • MLA gotcha: absorbed MLA never materialises keys, so Kimi K2 uses QK-Clip instead of QK-norm.
  • Not free: Cohere’s RNoPE design (Command A) dropped QK-norm: it flattened attention and hurt long-context retrieval.

Attention sinks → a learned sink logit

Attention sinks 2023 · Xiao et al., MIT / Meta · ICLR 2024 · arXiv:2309.17453 — Learned sink logit 2025 · OpenAI (gpt-oss) · arXiv:2508.10925 · Used in: gpt-oss, MiMo-V2-Flash (SWA layers), DeepSeek-V4

αtj=exp⁡(etj)exp⁡(zh)+∑r≤texp⁡(etr),∑j≤tαtj=1−exp⁡(zh)exp⁡(zh)+∑r≤texp⁡(etr)<1 \alpha_{tj}=\frac{\exp(e_{tj})}{\exp(z_h)+\sum_{r\le t}\exp(e_{tr})},\qquad \sum_{j\le t}\alpha_{tj}=1-\frac{\exp(z_h)}{\exp(z_h)+\sum_{r\le t}\exp(e_{tr})}<1

  • Why sinks appear: weights must sum to 1, so a head with nothing to read parks mass on early tokens. StreamingLLM keeps 4 of them (ch. 05).
  • The fix: one learned logit zhz_h per head joins the denominator. It has no value vector, so the head can attend to nothing.
  • Cost: one scalar per head. gpt-oss puts it in every layer; MiMo-V2-Flash only in SWA layers; DeepSeek-V4’s kernel takes attn_sink.
  • Compare: an output gate (next card) also removes the sink, without changing the softmax.

Gated attention: a sigmoid gate after SDPA

Gated attention 2025 · Qiu et al., Qwen (Alibaba) · NeurIPS 2025 Best Paper · arXiv:2505.06708 · Used in: Qwen3-Next, Qwen3.5 (attn_output_gate)

Y′=Y⊙σ(XWθ),Y=softmax⁡(QK⊤dh)V,out=WOY′ Y'=Y\odot\sigma(XW_\theta),\qquad Y=\operatorname{softmax}\Big(\frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_h}}\Big)V,\qquad \text{out}=W_O\,Y'

  • Why it works: a nonlinearity between WVW_V and WOW_O, plus query-dependent sparsity (mean gate 0.116, reported).
  • Cost: elementwise gating adds ≈201M parameters to the 15B MoE (headwise: 1.6M) and under 2% latency (reported).
  • Gate location: this gate rescales one token’s read. A state gate (αt\alpha_t in Gated DeltaNet) changes what every later query reads.
  • Gotcha: a gate near 0 does not skip work. SDPA has already run; only its output is suppressed.

03 · Share KV heads

Decode re-reads the whole cache for every new token. First lever: store fewer K/V heads per token and keep every query head.

MHA → GQA → MQA: one knob, HkvH_{kv}

MQA 2019 · Shazeer, Google · arXiv:1911.02150 — GQA 2023 · Ainslie et al., Google Research · EMNLP 2023 · arXiv:2305.13245 · Used in: PaLM, Falcon-7B, Gemma-2B (MQA); Llama 2-70B, Llama 3, Mistral 7B, Qwen2/3 (GQA)

ot,i=∑j≤tsoftmax⁡j(qt,i⊤kj,g(i)dh)vj,g(i),g(i)=⌊iHkvHq⌋ o_{t,i}=\sum_{j\le t}\operatorname{softmax}_j\!\Big(\frac{q_{t,i}^{\top}k_{j,g(i)}}{\sqrt{d_h}}\Big)\,v_{j,g(i)}, \qquad g(i)=\Big\lfloor \frac{i\,H_{kv}}{H_q}\Big\rfloor

  • Each query head keeps its own softmax. Shared K/V does not mean shared attention patterns.
  • Quality: uptrained GQA-8 lands close to MHA quality at near-MQA decode speed (reported, T5-XXL, GQA §4).
  • MHA → GQA conversion: mean-pool each group’s K/V projections, then uptrain for about 5% of the pre-training steps (GQA §2.1).

What it saves in real models

KV bytes per token=L⋅2Hkvdh⋅s \text{KV bytes per token}=L\cdot 2H_{kv}d_h\cdot s

  • Read it off config.json: num_hidden_layers × 2 × num_key_value_heads × head_dim × bytes.
  • Width, not length. Sharing shrinks each record. The number of records still grows with NN.
  • Batch multiplies it. 32 concurrent 8K requests on Llama-3-70B need 80 GiB of KV in BF16.

Why decode benefits: intensity = group size

I=FLOPsbytes≈4GNdh4Ndh=G=HqHkv I=\frac{\text{FLOPs}}{\text{bytes}}\approx\frac{4\,G\,N d_h}{4\,N d_h}=G=\frac{H_q}{H_{kv}}

  • At decode, bytes moved ≈ time. One KV byte feeds GG heads under GQA but only one under MHA.
  • Ridge point: H100 SXM ≈ 989 TFLOP/s ÷ 3.35 TB/s ≈ 295 FLOP/B. Attention decode sits far left of it.
  • Tensor-parallel gotcha: KV heads are split across GPUs. If HkvH_{kv} < TP degree, KV heads get replicated and the saving shrinks.
Where 4GNd_h comes from

Per query head: scores q⊤kjq^\top k_j for NN keys cost 2Ndh2Nd_h FLOPs, and the weighted sum ∑jpjvj\sum_j p_j v_j another 2Ndh2Nd_h. A group of GG heads does 4GNdh4GNd_h. The group reads NdhN d_h key elements and NdhN d_h value elements once: 4Ndh4Nd_h bytes in BF16.

K=V: one vector is both key and value

K=V 2026 · Gemma Team, Google · arXiv:2607.02770 — DeepSeek-AI · arXiv:2606.19348 — study: Kayyam et al. · ICML 2026 · arXiv:2606.04032 · Used in: Gemma 4 31B / 26B-A4B (global layers), DeepSeek-V4-Pro / Flash

uj=WKhj,kj=RoPE⁡j(norm⁡g(uj)),vj=norm⁡(uj) u_j=W_K h_j,\qquad k_j=\operatorname{RoPE}_j\big(\operatorname{norm}_g(u_j)\big),\qquad v_j=\operatorname{norm}(u_j)

  • Why it works: keys and values can share one subspace. Reported cost: +3.1% perplexity for −50% KV (Kayyam et al.).
  • Gotcha: a rotated vector is a poor value. Gemma 4 never rotates vv; DeepSeek-V4 rotates the output back by −t-t.
  • Numbers: Gemma 4 reports −37.5% global KV, not −50%. V4 caches one 512-element entry per token (one KV head).
  • Intensity doubles: the same bytes feed q⊤kq^\top k and ∑jpjvj\sum_j p_j v_j, so I≈2GI\approx 2G (next card).

Beyond GQA: MFA, GTA, GLA

MFA 2024 · Hu et al., StepFun · arXiv:2412.19255, Step-3 arXiv:2507.19427 — GTA / GLA 2025 · Zadouri, Strauss, Dao, Princeton · arXiv:2505.21487 · Used in: Step-3 (MFA)

I≈2Gm,G=Hqcached heads,m={2K≠V1tied or latent I\approx\frac{2G}{m},\qquad G=\frac{H_q}{\text{cached heads}},\qquad m=\begin{cases}2 & K\neq V\\ 1 & \text{tied or latent}\end{cases}

  • Lens: MHA ≈ 1, GQA ≈ GG, MQA ≈ HqH_q, MLA ≈ 2Hq2H_q (Zadouri et al., Table 1). Past the ridge, extra heads cost time.
  • MFA: one K and one V head of 256 serve 64 query heads with a rank-2048 query. Step-3 (8-bit KV): MFA 128, MLA 512 FLOP/B.
  • GTA: the tied state is the value; the key is its first half plus a shared RoPE half. Reported: GQA quality, about half the cache.
  • GLA: two latent heads instead of one, so TP=2 duplicates nothing: 640 vs 1,152 B/token/device (Table 5).

Cross-layer sharing: fewer layers own a cache

CLA 2024 · Brandon et al., MIT / IBM · NeurIPS 2024 · arXiv:2405.12981 — YOCO 2024 · Sun et al., Microsoft / Tsinghua · NeurIPS 2024 · arXiv:2405.05254 · Used in: Hunyuan-Large (CLA2), Gemma 3n, Gemma 4 E2B / E4B

  • Storage, not reads: every consumer layer still reads the shared cache.
  • Prefill wins: consumer layers produce no KV, so prompt tokens exit early (YOCO). Gemma 3n reports 2× prefill vs Gemma 3 4B.
  • Train it in: shared layers have no k_proj/v_proj (HF Gemma 4 code). Gemma 4 shares 20/35 (E2B) and 18/42 (E4B) layers.
  • Stacks: Hunyuan-Large combines GQA-8 with CLA2: about 95% below MHA (reported).

04 · Latent KV

05 · Fixed patterns

Most layers do not need the whole history. Fix in advance which keys each query may see, and those layers stop growing with NN.

Sparse Transformer → Longformer / BigBird: masks fixed by position

Sparse Transformer 2019 · Child et al., OpenAI · arXiv:1904.10509 — Longformer 2020 · Beltagy et al., AI2 · arXiv:2004.05150 — BigBird 2020 · Zaheer et al., Google · NeurIPS 2020 · arXiv:2007.14062 · Used in: GPT-3, gpt-oss (dense/banded alternation)

ot=∑j∈Atsoftmax⁡j∈At(qt⊤kjdh)vj,At depends on positions only, never on content o_t=\sum_{j\in A_t}\operatorname{softmax}_{j\in A_t}\!\Big(\frac{q_t^{\top}k_j}{\sqrt{d_h}}\Big)v_j, \qquad A_t \text{ depends on positions only, never on content}

  • Why it works: depth routes information. Band then stride connects any pair in two layers, at O(nn)O(n\sqrt n) cost.
  • Gotcha: strided routing failed on text (no periodic structure). The paper’s “fixed” variant uses blocks plus summary columns instead.
  • Used in: GPT-3 alternated dense and locally banded layers. gpt-oss does the same “following GPT-3”, band 128.
  • Not solved: global and random links are chosen before any query exists. Content-chosen reads: 06.

Sliding window + global layers in production

SWA : global 2023–26 · Mistral 7B arXiv:2310.06825 · Gemma 2 arXiv:2408.00118 · Gemma 3 arXiv:2503.19786 · gpt-oss arXiv:2508.10925 · MiMo-V2-Flash arXiv:2601.02780 · Used in: Gemma 2/3, gpt-oss, OLMo 3, MiMo-V2-Flash, DeepSeek-V4-Flash

  • Ring buffer: a local layer writes token tt into slot tmod⁡Wt \bmod W. Mistral 7B: 8× less cache at 32K.
  • Global layers set the bill. They grow with NN, and only they retrieve beyond WW.
  • Small windows need a sink. MiMo 32B ablation: WW = 128 beat 512 on RULER-32K (89.4 vs 84.7). See learned sinks.
  • Numbers: Gemma 3 report: global-only KV overhead about 60%, under 15% with 1,024-token windows.

Chunked local attention (Llama 4) vs sliding window

  • Compare: same cache cap as SWA with W=CW=C, half the pairs, but each chunk start is nearly blind locally.
  • Why chunks: the mask is block-diagonal, so each chunk is an ordinary causal attention and the cache empties at every boundary.
  • iRoPE: chunked layers keep RoPE; every 4th layer is global with no RoPE (positions).

StreamingLLM: window + 4 sink tokens

At={0,1,2,3}∪{t−W+1,…,t},pos(j)=rank of j inside the cache A_t=\{0,1,2,3\}\,\cup\,\{t-W+1,\dots,t\}, \qquad \text{pos}(j)=\text{rank of } j \text{ inside the cache}

  • Why it works: softmax must sum to 1, so heads park spare mass on the first tokens. Evict them and that mass lands elsewhere.
  • Numbers: 4 sinks suffice; 1–2 do not (§4.4). Up to 22.2× faster than a window with recomputation.
  • Not solved: stability, not memory. Recall drops to zero once the answer is farther back than the cache (App. C).
  • Learned version: one sink logit per head replaces the 4 tokens: sinks. Smarter eviction: 07.

06 · Sparse reads

Keep every record, but let each query read only a few: a cheap scorer picks them, and exact attention runs on the picks.

What shrinks: reads or records?

HBM=Nstored⋅brec,bytes per step=Nread⋅brec+scorer \text{HBM}=N_{\text{stored}}\cdot b_{\text{rec}}, \qquad \text{bytes per step}=N_{\text{read}}\cdot b_{\text{rec}}+\text{scorer}

  • Reversible: a later query can still reach every record. Only sparse reads and offload keep that property.
  • Cost: sparse reads cut bandwidth, not memory. The scorer still touches all NN records per query (last card).
  • Compare: fixed masks 05 · eviction 07 · fewer records 08 · offload 14.

Training-free prefill: MInference → XAttention

MInference 2024 · Jiang et al., Microsoft · NeurIPS 2024 · arXiv:2407.02490 — FlexPrefill 2025 · ICLR 2025 · arXiv:2502.20766 — XAttention 2025 · MIT · ICML 2025 · arXiv:2503.16428 · Used in: Qwen2.5-1M

Mij=1⇔j∈V∨(i−j)∈S,V,S=top columns / diagonals of softmax⁡(Q−64:K⊤) M_{ij}=1 \iff j\in V\ \lor\ (i-j)\in S, \qquad V,S=\text{top columns / diagonals of }\operatorname{softmax}(Q_{-64:}K^{\top})

  • Prefill only: the full KV cache is still written, and decode is untouched.
  • Per head: an offline search fixes each head’s pattern; indices are rebuilt for every input.
  • Threshold beats fixed k: FlexPrefill (JS-divergence test) and XAttention (antidiagonal sums) keep blocks until their mass reaches τ\tau.
  • Used in: Qwen2.5-1M serves Vertical-Slash prefill: reported 3–7× faster at 1M tokens.

Quest: page bounds choose what decode reads

Quest 2024 · Tang et al., MIT Han Lab · ICML 2024 · arXiv:2406.10774 · Used in: training-free decode on any MHA/GQA model

  • Why it works: each channel’s product peaks at an interval end, so UpU_p never underestimates a page.
  • Selection, not eviction: memory grows by 12.5% of K; bandwidth shrinks.
  • Gotcha: wide pages loosen the bound (page D: bound 5, true 3). Hence 16-token pages.
  • Lineage: cheap scorer → top-k → exact attention. DSA trains the scorer instead.

MoBA: route each query to a few blocks

MoBA 2025 · Lu et al., Moonshot AI · arXiv:2502.13189 · Used in: Kimi long-context service

st,b=qt⊤mean⁡j∈bkj,At=Top⁡k(st,:)∪current block (causal) s_{t,b}=q_t^{\top}\operatorname{mean}_{j\in b}k_j, \qquad A_t=\operatorname{Top}_k(s_{t,:})\cup\text{current block (causal)}

  • No router weights: the gate is qq against mean keys. Continued training adapts qq and kk to it.
  • Gotcha: a mean dilutes one sharp key in a 4,096-token block. MSA max-pools instead (last card).
  • Hybrid: the last 3 layers stay full attention; SFT’s masked prompt loss starved sparse layers of gradient.
  • Numbers: reported 6.5× prefill at 1M; RULER 128K 0.7818 vs 0.7849 full.

NSA: compress, select, slide

NSA 2025 · Yuan et al., DeepSeek-AI / PKU · ACL 2025 · arXiv:2502.11089 · Used in: research 27B MoE; selection reused by InfLLM-v2 (MiniCPM4.1)

ot=∑c∈{cmp,slc,win}gtcAttn⁡(qt,Ktc,Vtc),gtc=σ(MLP⁡(xt)) o_t=\sum_{c\in\{\text{cmp},\,\text{slc},\,\text{win}\}} g^c_t\,\operatorname{Attn}\big(q_t,\,K^c_t,\,V^c_t\big), \qquad g^c_t=\sigma(\operatorname{MLP}(x_t))

  • Free scorer: selection reuses the compression branch’s attention scores, so no separate indexer.
  • Group-shared: one top-16 per GQA group, so each KV block is loaded once for 16 heads.
  • Gotcha: three branches with their own KV projections. InfLLM-v2 reuses the dense KV: 96 blocks of 64 (≈6K tokens), no new parameters.
  • Bridge: the compression branch starts 08.

DSA: a lightning indexer picks 2,048 tokens

DSA 2025 · DeepSeek-AI · DeepSeek-V3.2 report · arXiv:2512.02556 · Used in: DeepSeek-V3.2(-Exp), GLM-5

It,s=∑j=164wt,jReLU⁡(qt,jI⋅ksI),At=Top⁡2048(It,:) I_{t,s}=\sum_{j=1}^{64} w_{t,j}\,\operatorname{ReLU}\big(q^{I}_{t,j}\cdot k^{I}_{s}\big), \qquad A_t=\operatorname{Top}_{2048}(I_{t,:})

  • ReLU per head, then weight. ReLU was chosen for throughput; the indexer runs in FP8.
  • Cost: main attention drops to O(Nk)O(Nk), but the indexer stays O(N2)O(N^2) and outweighs it at 128K (derived 3.8×).
  • Token-level, shared: one top-2,048 set per token for all 128 heads, so reads are gathers, not blocks.
  • Cache not smaller: indexer keys are stored too. Latent bytes read per layer at 128K: 1.3 MB vs 86 MB dense.

2026 indexers: same reads, cheaper scorer

  • MSA (MiniMax-M3, arXiv:2606.13392): top-16 blocks of 128 per GQA group, scored by max-pooling. Reported 28.4× less attention compute at 1M.
  • QSA (Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, arXiv:2608.30320): fills the 1-in-4 full-attention layers of a GDN hybrid (12).
  • IndexShare (GLM-5.2): IndexCache (arXiv:2603.12201) reuses top-k across layers: 75% fewer indexer computations.

07 · Evict from the cache

Selection reads fewer records but keeps them all. Eviction deletes records for good, so it must guess what future queries will need.

H2O: heavy hitters plus a recent window

H2O 2023 · Zhang et al., UT Austin / Stanford / Meta / CMU · NeurIPS 2023 · arXiv:2306.14048 · Used in: NVIDIA kvpress (ObservedAttentionPress); the standard eviction baseline

Sj←Sj+at,j,𝒦t=Recent⁡B/2(t)∪Top⁡B/2(S),j∉𝒦t⇒evicted for good S_j \leftarrow S_j + a_{t,j},\qquad \mathcal{K}_t=\operatorname{Recent}_{B/2}(t)\,\cup\,\operatorname{Top}_{B/2}(S),\qquad j\notin\mathcal{K}_t\ \Rightarrow\ \text{evicted for good}

  • Why it works: a few tokens collect most of the attention, and their accumulated score stays high across steps.
  • Gotcha: it needs at,ja_{t,j} at every step. Fused FlashAttention kernels never write it out.
  • Gotcha: early tokens have been summed for the most steps, so the score is biased toward the start of the context.
  • Numbers (reported): 20% budget. Up to 29× throughput vs DeepSpeed and Accelerate, and 3× vs FlexGen, on OPT-6.7B/30B.

SnapKV: the prompt’s tail votes once

SnapKV 2024 · Li et al., UIUC / Cohere · NeurIPS 2024 · arXiv:2404.14469 · Used in: NVIDIA kvpress (SnapKVPress); the scorer behind PyramidKV and Ada-KV

Cj=∑i∈obssoftmax⁡(Kqidh)j,𝒦(h)=Top⁡B(maxpool⁡7(C))∪obs C_j=\sum_{i\in\text{obs}}\operatorname{softmax}\!\Big(\frac{K q_i}{\sqrt{d_h}}\Big)_j,\qquad \mathcal{K}^{(h)}=\operatorname{Top}_B\big(\operatorname{maxpool}_7(C)\big)\cup\text{obs}

  • Once, after prefill: generated tokens are appended and never pruned, so long outputs still grow the cache.
  • Why pool: max-pooling also keeps the neighbours of a hit, so a multi-token string such as “order 7314” survives whole.
  • Gotcha: the votes come from the prompt’s last 32 tokens. If the real question arrives later, the wrong tokens voted.
  • Numbers (reported): 3.6× faster generation and 8.2× better memory efficiency at 16K. Up to 380K tokens fit on one A100-80GB.

PyramidKV and Ada-KV: where the budget goes

PyramidKV 2024 · Cai et al., UW–Madison / PKU / Microsoft · arXiv:2406.02069 — Ada-KV 2024 · Feng et al., USTC · NeurIPS 2025 · arXiv:2407.11550 · Used in: NVIDIA kvpress (PyramidKVPress, AdaKVPress)

PyramidKV:kℓ=k0−ℓL−1(k0−kL−1),kL−1=ktotβL,k0=2ktotL−kL−1Ada-KV:Bh=#{entries of head h in Top⁡B(C(1),…,C(H))} \begin{aligned} \text{PyramidKV:}\quad & k^{\ell}=k^{0}-\tfrac{\ell}{L-1}\big(k^{0}-k^{L-1}\big),\qquad k^{L-1}=\tfrac{k_{\text{tot}}}{\beta L},\qquad k^{0}=\tfrac{2k_{\text{tot}}}{L}-k^{L-1}\\ \text{Ada-KV:}\quad & B_h=\#\big\{\text{entries of head } h \text{ in } \operatorname{Top}_{B}\big(C^{(1)},\dots,C^{(H)}\big)\big\} \end{aligned}

  • Allocation, not scoring: both reuse SnapKV’s window scores. Only the share each layer or head gets changes.
  • Why a pyramid: attention is spread wide in lower layers and concentrated in upper layers, so upper layers need fewer entries.
  • Why per head: Ada-KV bounds the output error by the attention mass it drops. A pooled Top-B drops the least mass.
  • Numbers (reported): PyramidKV matches full KV on LongBench with 12% of the cache. With 128 entries, LLaMA-3-70B scores 100.0 on Needle.

KVzip: keep what the model needs to repeat the context

KVzip 2025 · Kim et al., Seoul National University / NAVER AI Lab · NeurIPS 2025 (oral) · arXiv:2505.23416 · Used in: NVIDIA kvpress (KVzipPress)

sj=max⁡g≤G,i∈repeatai,j(g),𝒦=Top⁡r%(sover all KV heads) s_j=\max_{g\le G,\;i\in\text{repeat}} a^{(g)}_{i,j},\qquad \mathcal{K}=\operatorname{Top}_{r\%}\big(s\ \text{over all KV heads}\big)

  • Query-agnostic: the prompt “Repeat the previous context:” stands in for every future question, so one pruned cache serves many queries.
  • Cost: one extra pass over the context in 2K-token chunks, which adds roughly one more prefill’s worth of causal attention.
  • Compare: the authors report that query-aware eviction degrades in multi-query use even at a 90% budget.
  • GQA: the max runs over the GG query heads of each KV head, so a shared KV pair is scored once.

DuoAttention and RazorAttention: evict per head

RazorAttention 2024 · Tang et al., Huawei · ICLR 2025 · arXiv:2407.15891 — DuoAttention 2024 · Xiao et al., MIT / NVIDIA · ICLR 2025 · arXiv:2410.10819 · Used in: NVIDIA kvpress (DuoAttentionPress)

oh=αhAttn⁡full+(1−αh)Attn⁡sink+recent,ℒ=‖hfull−hα‖22+λ‖α‖1,λ=0.05 o_h=\alpha_h\,\operatorname{Attn}_{\text{full}}+(1-\alpha_h)\,\operatorname{Attn}_{\text{sink+recent}},\qquad \mathcal{L}=\lVert h_{\text{full}}-h_{\alpha}\rVert_2^2+\lambda\lVert\alpha\rVert_1,\ \ \lambda=0.05

  • Static, per head: decided once offline, so decode needs no scorer and keeps standard kernels (unlike H2O).
  • Gotcha: under GQA a “head” is a KV head. Llama-3-8B needs 50% retrieval heads, while MHA Llama-2-7B needs 25%.
  • Streaming head: a per-head StreamingLLM cache. A trained sink logit removes the need to store sink tokens.
  • RazorAttention: training-free choice of heads. It folds dropped tokens into one compensation token and reports over 70% KV reduction.

DMC → DMS: learn what to merge or evict

DMC 2024 · Nawrot et al., NVIDIA / University of Edinburgh · ICML 2024 · arXiv:2403.09636 — DMS 2025 · Łańcucki et al., NVIDIA / University of Edinburgh · NeurIPS 2025 · arXiv:2506.05345 · Used in: NVIDIA checkpoints Llama-2-7B/13B-DMC-4x/8x, Qwen3-8B-DMS-8x

DMC:αt=1:klast←zklast+ωtktz+ωt,z←z+ωt;αt=0:append ktDMS:αt=1⇒evict (kt,vt) at step t+w \begin{aligned} \text{DMC:}\quad & \alpha_t=1:\ k_{\text{last}}\leftarrow\frac{z\,k_{\text{last}}+\omega_t k_t}{z+\omega_t},\ \ z\leftarrow z+\omega_t;\qquad \alpha_t=0:\ \text{append } k_t\\ \text{DMS:}\quad & \alpha_t=1\ \Rightarrow\ \text{evict } (k_t,v_t) \text{ at step } t+w \end{aligned}

  • No new parameters: αt\alpha_t and DMC’s ωt\omega_t come from repurposed neurons of qtq_t or ktk_t. Values are merged the same way as keys.
  • Why delay: during the ww steps before eviction, later tokens can still read the entry and absorb what it holds.
  • Cost: to reach 8×, DMC needs about 44K training steps and DMS about 1K, using logit distillation from the original model.
  • Hyper-scaling: the same KV reads buy longer reasoning traces. DMS reports higher AIME 24, GPQA and LiveCodeBench scores on Qwen-R1.

Pitfalls: eviction cannot be undone

ℰt⊆ℰt+1,a query at step t can use token j only if j∉ℰt \mathcal{E}_t\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{t+1},\qquad \text{a query at step } t \text{ can use token } j \text{ only if } j\notin\mathcal{E}_t

  • Not solved: selection can choose again for each query, but eviction cannot. Always score the queries that arrive after compression.
  • Gotcha: if the question is still in the compressed prompt, SnapKV-style scorers look better than they are (NVIDIA kvpress README).
  • Gotcha: instructions fade unevenly, and system-prompt leakage rises under compression (ACL 2026).
  • Stacks with: KV quantization keeps every record and cuts bytes per record.

08 · Compress the sequence

Eviction deletes positions. Compression merges each run of m tokens into one learned record, so the cache grows as N/m instead of N.

Compressive Transformer: pool old memories, don’t drop them

Compressive Transformer 2019 · Rae et al., DeepMind · ICLR 2020 · arXiv:1911.05507 · Used in: research only; the ancestor of the HCA idea, and the paper that introduced PG-19

cm←fc(moldest ns)∈ℝ⌊ns/c⌋×d,range=L(nm+cncm) \text{cm}\leftarrow f_c\big(m_{\text{oldest }n_s}\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{\lfloor n_s/c\rfloor\times d},\qquad \text{range}=L\,(n_m+c\,n_{cm})

  • Why it works: pooling old memories at rate cc instead of dropping them lets a fixed slot budget cover cc times more history.
  • Compare: it reads every pooled record densely, like HCA, with no top-k selection, unlike CSA.
  • Converts from: the “most-used” fcf_c keeps the most-attended memories, which is H2O-style eviction.
  • Numbers (reported): 0.97 bpc on Enwik8 and 17.1 ppl on WikiText-103. On PG-19, 33.6 ppl vs 36.3 for Transformer-XL.

Gist tokens → Activation Beacon: learned summary tokens

Gist tokens 2023 · Mu, Li, Goodman, Stanford · NeurIPS 2023 · arXiv:2304.08467 — Activation Beacon 2024 · Zhang et al., BAAI / Renmin University · ICLR 2025 · arXiv:2401.03462 · Used in: research (FlagEmbedding release)

gist: Mij=0for i>G,j∈P;beacon: |KV|≈Nα+w,α∈{2,4,8,16,32} \text{gist: } M_{ij}=0\ \ \text{for } i>G,\ j\in P;\qquad \text{beacon: } |\text{KV}|\approx\frac{N}{\alpha}+w,\quad \alpha\in\{2,4,8,16,32\}

  • Learned records: special tokens are trained to absorb a span. The span’s own KV is then dropped.
  • Gist vs Beacon: gist squeezes one prompt into a few tokens. Beacon compresses every chunk of a long input, in every layer.
  • Train vs. infer: Beacon trains only new beacon projections and keeps the LLM frozen. A random α\alpha per chunk lets one model serve several ratios.
  • Not solved: both are added after pretraining. CSA and HCA build compression into pretraining.

NSA’s compression branch: the bridge

NSA 2025 · Yuan et al., DeepSeek / Peking University · ACL 2025 · arXiv:2502.11089 · Used in: research (27B MoE in the paper); full card in 06

K̃tcmp={φ(kid+1:id+l):0≤i≤⌊t−ld⌋},l=32,d=16 \tilde K^{\text{cmp}}_t=\Big\{\varphi\big(k_{id+1:\,id+l}\big)\;:\;0\le i\le\Big\lfloor\frac{t-l}{d}\Big\rfloor\Big\},\qquad l=32,\ d=16

  • Bridge: overlapping windows (l=2dl = 2d) and a learned pooler φ\varphi are the two ingredients CSA keeps.
  • Two jobs: compressed keys are read densely. Their scores, summed per 64-token block and per GQA group, pick the selected blocks.
  • Not solved: the raw KV stays for the selection and window branches, so storage grows. CSA drops it.
  • Numbers: at 64K a query reads about 4,095 compressed keys, 16 × 64 selected tokens and a 512 window: 8.6% of the context.

CSA: compress by 4, then select top-k

CSA (Compressed Sparse Attention) 2026 · DeepSeek-AI · arXiv:2606.19348 · Used in: DeepSeek-V4-Flash, DeepSeek-V4-Pro

ci=∑j∈blki−1∪blkisoftmax⁡j(zj+Bj)⊙xj,At=Top⁡k(It,:)∪win128,|blk|=m=4 c_i=\sum_{j\in\text{blk}_{i-1}\cup\,\text{blk}_i}\operatorname{softmax}_j\big(z_j+B_j\big)\odot x_j,\qquad A_t=\operatorname{Top}_k\big(I_{t,:}\big)\cup\text{win}_{128},\qquad |\text{blk}|=m=4

  • Two budgets at once: stored records drop to N/4N/4, and reads are capped at kk = 1,024 (Pro) or 512 (Flash).
  • Overlap: entry ii sees blocks i−1i-1 and ii through separate projections, yet one entry is emitted per 4 tokens.
  • Indexer: DSA-style, 64 heads × 128 dims, with QK cached and multiplied in FP4. kk is below V3.2’s 2,048 because entries span several tokens.
  • Storage: one 512-dim entry serves as both K and V. RoPE dims stay BF16, the rest FP8. Each head has a sink logit.

HCA: compress by 128, read everything

HCA (Heavily Compressed Attention) 2026 · DeepSeek-AI · arXiv:2606.19348 · Used in: DeepSeek-V4-Flash, DeepSeek-V4-Pro

ci=∑j∈blkisoftmax⁡j(zj+Bj)⊙xj,|blki|=m′=128,At={cs:s<⌊t/128⌋}∪win128 c_i=\sum_{j\in\text{blk}_i}\operatorname{softmax}_j\big(z_j+B_j\big)\odot x_j,\quad |\text{blk}_i|=m'=128,\qquad A_t=\{c_s: s<\lfloor t/128\rfloor\}\cup\text{win}_{128}

  • No scorer: every finished entry is read, so there is no indexer cost and no top-k miss.
  • Cost: reads still grow as N/128N/128. At 1M a query reads 8,320 entries, against CSA’s fixed 1,152.
  • Why pair them: CSA keeps finer entries but must choose among them. HCA sees all of history, coarsely. V4 alternates the two by layer.
  • Compare: the Compressive Transformer also read pooled memories densely, with cc = 2–4 and a bounded memory.

The KV cache grows with every token. Drop the softmax and the whole history folds into one fixed dv×dkd_v\times d_k matrix per head.

  • Why it works: without the softmax, (QK⊤)V=Q(K⊤V)(QK^\top)V=Q(K^\top V). Decode costs O(dvdk)O(d_vd_k) per token at any NN.
  • Gotcha: this is a different model, not a faster softmax. The 2020 version used a feature map ϕ\phi and a normalizer zt⊤ϕ(qt)z_t^{\top}\phi(q_t).
  • Normalizer dropped: it made gradients unstable. Modern layers use L2-normalized q,kq,k and output RMSNorm instead. mLSTM still keeps one.
  • Not solved: pure addition never forgets or overwrites. Every later card changes how SS is updated.

10 · The delta rule

Additive states can only pile up evidence. The delta rule reads what the state predicts for a key and overwrites only the error.

DeltaNet: read, compare, overwrite

DeltaNet 2021 · Schlag, Irie, Schmidhuber (IDSIA) · ICML 2021 · arXiv:2102.11174 — Parallel DeltaNet 2024 · Yang, Wang, Zhang, Shen, Kim · NeurIPS 2024 · arXiv:2406.06484 · Used in: the base of every card below

  • Why it works: it is one gradient step on 12‖Skt−vt‖2\tfrac12\lVert Sk_t-v_t\rVert^2 (the LMS rule). Linear attention only adds. More in ch 11.
  • Gotcha: the edit is exact only for orthogonal keys. A query that overlaps ktk_t shifts too: 4.24 → 6.36 in the toy.
  • Train vs. infer: q,kq,k are L2-normalized and β∈(0,1)\beta\in(0,1). Chunked training turns products of (I−βkk⊤)(I-\beta kk^\top) into a WY/UT form.

  • Why it works: α\alpha clears a whole head at once; β\beta edits one association. Mamba-2 had only the first, DeltaNet only the second.
  • Order: decay first, so the residual is read from the faded state: 1.6, not 2. The other order is a different rule (sum form).
  • Numbers: count state per value head. Qwen3-Next pairs 32 value heads with 16 query/key heads.
  • Gotcha: a scalar α\alpha also fades unrelated associations: 4 → 3.2 in the toy.

12 · Hybrid stacks

A fixed state compresses but cannot look things up. Production models keep a few full-attention layers and make the rest cheap.

Why hybrid: state is cheap, retrieval is not

  • Why it works: recurrent layers carry a summary; a few attention layers keep every token for exact lookup (11 · Limits).

13 · Kernels

Same exact attention, different schedule: kernels decide how many bytes cross HBM and how many SMs stay busy.
